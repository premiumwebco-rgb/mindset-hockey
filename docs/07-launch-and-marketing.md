# Launch Plan & Marketing Strategy

## Part I — Launch

### Phase 0 · Weeks 1–2 — Prove demand before you build

**Goal: 300 emails and 25 waitlist deposits before writing app code.**

| Do | Detail |
|---|---|
| Ship the PageFlow landing page | `pageflow/index.html` — live in an afternoon |
| Publish the story film | 90s, founder to camera + footage + the two injuries |
| Launch the free assessment | 12 questions → scored report → email capture |
| Post 10 free breakdowns | IG Reels + TikTok + YouTube Shorts |
| DM 50 hockey parents you already know | Not a pitch. "I'm building this — would you look at it?" |
| Open a $1 waitlist deposit | Credited to month 1. A dollar filters tire-kickers from buyers. |

**Kill criterion:** if 300 emails produce fewer than 15 deposits, the offer is wrong. Fix the offer, not the funnel.

### Phase 1 · Weeks 3–8 — Founding Members

**Goal: 50 paying members.**

- Build MVP: auth, Stripe, library, Shot Mechanics Course, Mindset Track, drills, dashboard.
- **Founding Member offer:** $35/mo Basic or $59/mo Advanced, *locked for life*, first 50 only, cancel anytime.
  - Real scarcity: the cap exists because coach time is finite. Never fake the number.
- Record the core content in two focused blocks:
  - Block A (1 week): Shot Mechanics Course — 7 modules, ~40 min total.
  - Block B (1 week): Mindset Track — 12 lessons, ~8 min each.
- Onboard every founding member personally. A 15-minute call with each of the first 50 is worth more than any ad. It produces your testimonials, your roadmap, and your objection list.

### Phase 2 · Weeks 9–14 — Build the moat

**Goal: 150 members, first before/after proof asset.**

- Ship video analysis + coach console + pro breakdown library.
- Every Advanced member submits in week 1. Turn the first 20 reviews around in <48h to set the standard.
- **Capture the first before/after at day 30.** This single asset will out-convert everything else you make.
- Launch the referral program: refer a teammate → both get a free month.

### Phase 3 · Weeks 15–24 — Scale

**Goal: 400 members.**

- Weekly plan engine, PWA, team/association licences.
- First Summer Intensive cohort ($297).
- Hire/train a second reviewer at ~250 Advanced members.

## Part II — Marketing Strategy

### The Flywheel

```
Free breakdown content (IG/TikTok/YT)
        │  builds trust at zero cost
        ▼
Free assessment  ──▶  Email list
        │                  │
        │                  ▼
        │            Nurture sequence (story-led)
        ▼                  │
Membership ◀───────────────┘
        │
        ▼
Member results  ──▶  Before/after proof  ──▶  feeds content
        │
        ▼
Word of mouth in the rink lobby  ──▶  new members
```

The rink-lobby loop is the real engine. Hockey associations are small, dense networks. **Ten delighted families in one association will out-perform a $5,000 ad budget.** Every product decision should be evaluated on "does this make a parent tell another parent?"

### Channel Plan

**1. Short-form video — top of funnel (daily, non-negotiable)**

Highest-performing formats, in order:
1. **"Why your shot is slow" mechanical breakdowns** — freeze-frame + arrows. Instantly useful, instantly shareable.
2. **Before/after side-by-sides** — yours first, then members'.
3. **"Nobody tells 14-year-olds this"** mindset takes to camera.
4. **The injury story, retold in pieces** — the most-saved content you will make.
5. **Pro breakdowns** — NHL clip + one specific coaching point.

Rules: hook in 1.5 seconds. One idea per video. Text on screen (most watch muted). No CTA in the first 20 videos — earn the audience first.

**2. YouTube — trust builder (2×/week, 8–15 min)**

Long-form is where a parent decides you're real. Titles that work:
- "How I went from A hockey to Junior A (the actual system)"
- "The 7 things I look for in a shot"
- "What I'd do if I had one off-season left"
- "I broke my rib twice. Here's what it taught me about development."

**3. SEO — compounding (2 articles/week)**

Priority clusters:
- `how to shoot harder in hockey` · `hockey wrist shot technique` · `why is my shot slow`
- `how to make AAA hockey` · `how to get scouted for junior hockey` · `hockey tryout tips`
- `hockey confidence` · `hockey mental training` · `my son lost confidence in hockey` ← **least competitive, highest intent, and exactly our wedge**
- `hockey training at home` · `off ice shooting drills` · `hockey stickhandling drills`
- Programmatic: `/drills/[skill]-drills-for-[age]-year-olds` — hundreds of long-tail pages

**4. Email — where the sale happens**

*Welcome sequence (5 emails after the assessment):*
1. **Day 0** — Your report + the one thing to fix first *(pure value, no pitch)*
2. **Day 2** — The story: 16U A, two broken ribs, Junior A
3. **Day 4** — Why talent isn't the variable *(the philosophy)*
4. **Day 6** — Inside the system: the Six Pillars *(soft offer)*
5. **Day 8** — "Here's what it costs, and what it doesn't" *(direct offer + honest guarantee)*

*Ongoing:* Tuesday development tip (value), Friday member spotlight (proof). Two emails a week, forever.

**5. Facebook parent groups — highest conversion, lowest tolerance for selling**

Rule: give for 30 days before you ever mention the platform. Answer questions with real depth. When someone asks "how do I help my son's shot," write the actual answer — 300 words, no link. The link comes from the DMs that follow.

**6. Associations & teams — the leverage play**

Offer a free 45-minute mindset session to any team. Zero cost, builds relationships with coaches, and coaches are the single strongest referral source in hockey. Convert to team licences ($1,500–4,000/season).

**7. Paid — only after organic works**

Do not run ads until organic converts at >4%. Then:
- Retarget site visitors and video viewers (cheapest, highest ROAS)
- Meta lookalikes off the buyer list
- Geo-target hockey-dense markets: MN, MI, MA, NY, IL, CO, and Ontario/Alberta/BC
- Target CAC ≤ $120 against a $730 LTV

### Ethical Marketing Standard

Written down so it survives growth pressure:

1. No promise or implication of placement at any level.
2. No fake timers, fake seat counts, or fabricated testimonials.
3. No marketing that trades on a child's disappointment or a parent's guilt.
4. Results disclaimers on every testimonial.
5. Cancel in two clicks, always.
6. If a family can't afford it, tell them what to do for free. Some of them will come back with money; all of them will tell someone.

This is not softness. In a market that runs on word of mouth inside small associations, integrity is the cheapest customer acquisition channel available.
