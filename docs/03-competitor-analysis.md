# Competitor Analysis

## 1. The Landscape

| Competitor | Price | Core offer | Strength | Exploitable weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Project Hockey** | $19.99/mo | 3,000+ sessions, player + goalie skill sets | Volume; "most affordable on the planet" | Overwhelming. No path, no coach, no story. Cheap is their whole identity — they cannot move upmarket. |
| **iTrain Hockey (iTH+)** | $29.99/mo · $179.99/yr | 600+ videos, app-based | Polished app, brand recognition | Library, not a system. No human feedback. Generic instructors. |
| **Online Hockey Training** | ~$20–30/mo | 1,200+ at-home drills | At-home focus, strong SEO | Drill-dump model. No mindset, no analysis, no personalization. |
| **Hockey Training (hockeytraining.tv)** | $19.99/mo, 7-day free | Stickhandling sessions, challenges, off-ice strength | Strength/conditioning depth, challenge mechanic | Skews physical training. Weak on shot mechanics and psychology. |
| **How To Hockey — The Pond** (Coach Jeremy) | ~$20–30/mo | All-in-one training platform | Massive YouTube audience → cheap CAC | Beginner-skewed. Broad, not elite. Personality-led but not journey-led. |
| **Local private skills coaches** | $100–150/hr | 1:1 on-ice | Real feedback, real relationship | Expensive, capacity-limited, geographically limited, no homework between sessions. |
| **YouTube / free content** | $0 | Everything and nothing | Free, infinite | No order, no accountability, no assessment, no answer to "what should *my kid* do next?" |

## 2. The Structural Gap

Plot the market on two axes:

```
                    HIGH PERSONALIZATION
                            │
        Private skills      │      ◆ MINDSET HOCKEY
        coach ($100-150/hr) │        ($50-80/mo)
        (local, capacity-   │        Video review + assessment
         limited)           │        + a story + a system
                            │
   LOW ────────────────────┼──────────────────────── HIGH
   SCALE                    │                        SCALE
                            │
        Local camps         │      Project Hockey, iTrain,
        Clinics             │      Online Hockey Training,
                            │      The Pond, YouTube
                            │      ($0-30/mo, library model)
                    LOW PERSONALIZATION
```

**Everyone at scale competes on library size. Everyone with personalization is trapped in a 30-mile radius.**

The upper-right quadrant — personalized feedback delivered at software margins — is open. It is open because it requires a coach willing to actually watch video, and library companies structurally will not do that at $19.99/mo.

## 3. Three Wedges Nobody Else Owns

### Wedge 1 — The Recent, Verifiable Journey
Every competitor is either a retired pro (relatable to nobody), a fitness coach (relatable but not a hockey player), or an anonymous brand. **Mindset Hockey is the only platform where the person teaching just made the jump the customer's kid is trying to make.** A → AAA → Junior A, with injuries in the middle. That is not marketing; it is the product.

*A 12-year-old does not see themselves in a 45-year-old ex-pro. They see themselves in an 18-year-old who was A-level three years ago.*

### Wedge 2 — Mindset as a Trained Skill, Not a Slogan
Every competitor mentions "mental toughness" in a bullet and never teaches it. Nobody has a structured mindset curriculum: bounce-back protocols, pre-game routines, confidence after a bad shift, handling a coach who doesn't play you, coming back from injury.

Injury recovery in particular is **completely unowned** in this market, and it is the highest-empathy moment in a hockey family's year. The founder broke his rib twice. That is a curriculum.

### Wedge 3 — Human Video Analysis at a Software Price
$80/mo for two coach-reviewed shot breakdowns is cheaper than one lesson and impossible for a $19.99 library to match. It also produces the single most shareable artifact in the business: an annotated before/after of a real kid.

## 4. Competitive Response Playbook

| If a competitor... | We... |
|---|---|
| Drops price to $9.99 | Do nothing. Race to the bottom is their war. Raise the perceived value of coach access instead. |
| Adds video analysis | Point to turnaround time and reviewer identity. Theirs will be outsourced and generic; ours is named, on-camera, and rubric-scored. |
| Copies the founder-story angle | They can't. It's a fact about a person, not a positioning choice. |
| Outspends us on ads | Lean harder into associations, teams, and rink word-of-mouth — channels money can't buy. |

## 5. What to Steal (openly)

- **Hockey Training's "challenges"** → our streaks + weekly plan completion mechanic.
- **The Pond's YouTube flywheel** → free breakdown content as the top of funnel. Non-negotiable.
- **iTrain's app polish** → mobile-first dashboard; hockey kids are on phones.
- **Project Hockey's free trial** → 7-day free trial on Basic; 14-day money-back on Advanced.

## 6. Pricing Position Statement (for the sales page)

> A private skills coach costs $100–150 an hour and sees your son twice a month.
> Mindset Hockey is $80 a month and works with him every day.
> **We are not competing with the $20 app. We are competing with the lesson you already pay for.**
