# Market Research — Youth Hockey Development

## 1. Market Size and Spend

| Data point | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Avg. annual cost, youth ice hockey (all levels) | **~$2,583/yr** | PlaygroundEquipment.com |
| Tier 2 (house/A/AA — where most players are), 2025 season | **~$2,448/yr** | PlayMetrics 10-yr pricing benchmarks |
| Tier 1 (AAA) | **~$7,055/yr** | PlayMetrics |
| Elite travel programs | **$10,000+/yr** | Jersey Watch |
| Regional variance | Arizona families pay **13×** what Alaska families pay for Tier 2 | PlayMetrics |
| Odds of a youth player reaching the NHL | **<0.11%** | Stickhandling PRO |
| NHL active rosters that played NCAA hockey | **~33%** | College ice hockey statistics |

**The strategic read:** the money is already committed. Hockey families are not deciding *whether* to spend on development — they are deciding *where*. A $50–80/mo membership is 1–3% of a AAA family's annual outlay and less than the cost of a single private skills session in most markets. **We are not asking for new budget. We are asking for a reallocation of existing budget toward the part nobody is coaching: the mind and the mechanics between sessions.**

## 2. Buyer Segments

### Primary: The Invested Parent (85% of revenue)

- Ages 38–52. Household income $90K–$250K.
- Child is 10–18, playing A / AA / AAA or trying to reach the next tier.
- Already paying for: team fees, travel, equipment, and usually 1–2 private/skills sessions a month.
- Consumes: team GroupMe/Slack, Facebook hockey-parent groups, Instagram/TikTok hockey accounts, rink-side conversation.
- **Buys emotionally, justifies rationally.** Needs a story to believe and a system to point at.

**Three sub-personas:**

**A. "The Frustrated Investor" — highest-intent buyer**
Spent $8K last season. Kid didn't move up. Doesn't know if the problem is coaching, effort, the politics, or talent. Feels like they're pouring money into a hole. *Core question: "Am I wasting my money?"*
→ Sell: **clarity and a plan.** The assessment + weekly plan is the hook.

**B. "The Believer" — best long-term member**
Kid loves hockey and works hard. Parent wants to give them every legitimate edge without becoming *that* parent. *Core question: "Am I doing enough for my kid?"*
→ Sell: **structure and peace of mind.** Progress tracking is the retention hook.

**C. "The Gatekeeper Beater" — highest-urgency buyer**
Kid is close. Tryouts, showcases, or Junior camps are 3–9 months out. Convinced their kid is being overlooked. *Core question: "How do we get seen?"*
→ Sell: **video analysis and the pro breakdown library.** Advanced tier, immediately.

### Secondary: The Player (drives retention, not purchase)

- 13–18, self-motivated, watches hockey content daily.
- Will not swipe the card, but will *ask for it* — and will cancel it by not showing up.
- Motivated by: looking good, feeling capable, beating their own numbers, being trusted by coaches.
- **Design implication:** the parent buys the dashboard; the *player* has to want to open the app. Streaks, scores, and side-by-side pro comparison exist for the kid.

## 3. Hockey Parent Psychology

Grounded in the applied sport-psychology literature and hockey-parent community research.

### Their Fears

1. **"We're spending all this money for nothing."** Documented: the financial cost of youth sport participation is an identified source of parental stress and a barrier to enjoyment (*Journal of Applied Sport Psychology*, 2022).
2. **"My kid is falling behind and we won't notice until it's too late."** Parents carry *anticipatory stress* about their child's long-term trajectory — skill development, team placement, future opportunities — and this forward-looking anxiety intensifies reactions to individual games and decisions.
3. **"It's political and we're on the wrong side of it."** Ice-time jealousy ("Why is Jimmy getting more ice time than Johnny?") is one of the most commonly documented flashpoints in minor hockey.
4. **"My kid will quit and regret it."** ~70% of kids leave organized sport by 13 across sports; specialization pressure in hockey is well documented.
5. **"I'm becoming the parent I hate."** Real and rarely spoken. Parents know that visible frustration is internalized by kids as anxiety and fear of failure — and they hate themselves for it.

### Their Frustrations

- Team practices are 60 minutes of standing in lines. Real development doesn't happen there.
- Private coaches cost $100–150/hr, are booked out, and give no homework.
- YouTube is infinite, free, and unstructured — no path, no order, no accountability.
- Nobody will tell them the truth about where their kid actually stands.
- Their kid trains hard and still doesn't perform under pressure. Nobody coaches *that*.
- Feedback from coaches is vague: "compete harder," "want it more." Uncoachable advice.

### Their Goals

- Kid makes the next team. (Stated.)
- Kid gets seen by the right people. (Stated.)
- Kid learns something from hockey that outlasts hockey — discipline, resilience, leadership. (Deeply felt, rarely stated out loud, and **this is the highest-leverage message in the entire brand.**)
- The car ride home stops being tense.
- Their money produces something visible.

### Their Buying Triggers

| Trigger | Timing | Message that converts |
|---|---|---|
| **Tryout/cut disappointment** | Mar–May, Aug–Sep | "The gap isn't talent. Here's what it actually is." |
| **A bad stretch of games** | In-season | "Confidence is trainable. Here's the routine." |
| **Injury / time off the ice** | Anytime | **Founder's rib injuries — highest-empathy story asset.** "The time off is when I got better." |
| **Seeing another kid jump a level** | Anytime | "That kid isn't more talented. He has a system." |
| **Off-season starting** | Apr–Jun | "12 weeks decides next season." |
| **A coach says 'he needs to shoot better'** | Anytime | Shot Mechanics Course, direct offer |
| **Junior/prep camp invite** | Nov–Mar | Advanced tier, video analysis, urgency real |

### Objections (and the honest answer)

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| "$80/mo is a lot on top of everything." | It's less than one private lesson, and it works 30 days a month instead of 60 minutes. |
| "He's already got a skills coach." | Good. This is what he does the other 29 days. We make your skills coach more effective, not redundant. |
| "You're 19. What do you know?" | I'm not telling you what worked 20 years ago. I'm telling you what worked *last season*, at the level your son is trying to reach. |
| "Free stuff exists on YouTube." | So does every workout ever filmed, and most people are still out of shape. The value isn't the drill. It's the order, the plan, and someone watching. |
| "Will this get him to Junior?" | No one can promise that, and anyone who does is lying to you. What I can promise is a better shot, a clearer plan, and a kid who handles a bad game better. |

## 4. Content Consumption Behaviour

- **Instagram Reels / TikTok:** where discovery happens. Shot-mechanic breakdowns and before/after clips perform best.
- **YouTube:** where trust is built. 8–15 min breakdowns; highest intent traffic.
- **Facebook Groups:** where parents talk to each other. Highest-conversion referral channel; also the most hostile to overt selling.
- **Email:** where the sale happens. Hockey parents over 40 open email.
- **Rink lobbies:** the real distribution channel. Word of mouth in a hockey association is exponential and free.

## 5. Timing / Seasonality

| Window | Parent state | Play |
|---|---|---|
| **Aug–Sep** | Tryout anxiety peak | Highest-converting window of the year. Push hard. |
| **Oct–Dec** | In-season grind | Mindset + game-habits content. Retention focus. |
| **Jan–Mar** | Playoffs, Junior camps, evaluations | Advanced tier / video analysis peak. |
| **Apr–May** | Cuts, disappointment, reflection | **Second-highest conversion window.** Empathy-led messaging. |
| **Jun–Aug** | Off-season training | Summer Intensive, flagship product season. |
