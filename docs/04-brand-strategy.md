# Brand Strategy — MINDSET HOCKEY

## 1. Name & Rationale

**MINDSET HOCKEY**

Chosen because it does three jobs at once:
1. **States the wedge in the name.** Every competitor is "[Verb] Hockey" or "Hockey [Noun]" about *skills*. This one is about the thing that actually separated the founder from the players he passed.
2. **SEO-native.** "Hockey mindset," "hockey mental training," "hockey confidence" are all searched and all under-served.
3. **Extensible.** Mindset Hockey Academy, Mindset Hockey Pro, The Mindset Method, Mindset Camp.

**Lockup:** `MINDSET` in heavy condensed uppercase, `HOCKEY` in lighter tracked-out uppercase beneath or beside it. Wordmark only — no puck, no stick, no crossed sticks. Every hockey brand uses crossed sticks. The absence of a hockey cliché is what makes it read as premium.

**Domains to acquire:** mindsethockey.com / .co / trainmindsethockey.com / mindsethockey.ca
**Handles:** @mindsethockey across IG, TikTok, YouTube, X.

## 2. Brand Promise

> **Talent helps. Mindset, discipline, and consistent work change careers.**

This is the line on the hero, in the email signature, on the merch, and at the end of every video. It is also the thesis of the founder's actual life, which is why it will never feel like a slogan.

## 3. Brand Story (canonical version — use everywhere)

I was a 16U A player. Not the fastest. Not the most skilled. Not on anyone's list.

Then I broke my rib. And a year later, I broke it again.

Both times I lost months. Both times I sat in the stands and watched the game move on without me. That is where most players quietly disappear — not because they can't play, but because the time away breaks something in how they see themselves.

I decided to use it instead. If I couldn't skate, I could learn. I studied shot mechanics frame by frame. I built a training system I could run at home. I worked on the part of my game nobody was coaching: how I thought, how I responded to a bad shift, how I showed up on the days I didn't feel like it.

I came back different. Not more talented — more reliable. Coaches started using me in the moments that mattered. I was named an alternate captain. I moved up to AAA. And I signed with a Junior A organization.

Nothing about that was talent. All of it was repeatable.

**That system is what this platform is.**

### Story rules
- Never inflate it. No "went from nobody to superstar." The truth is more persuasive than the exaggeration.
- Always name the injuries. The setback is the credibility. A clean rise story is unrelatable; a recovery story is a mirror.
- Always land on *repeatable*. The point of the story is not "look at me." It is "this can be copied."
- Always credit the parents in the version aimed at parents. They funded it. Say so.

## 4. Brand Personality

|---|---|
| Direct | Loud |
| Disciplined | Militant |

**Voice test:** Would a respected junior coach say this in a quiet conversation after practice? If it sounds like an Instagram motivation account, cut it.

### Vocabulary

**Use:** compete level · habits · reps · reliable · trusted · earned · the standard · process · film · the little things · bounce back · coachable · consistency

**Ban:** grind (overused) · beast mode · dominate · elite mindset™ · alpha · secrets · hack · guaranteed · anything implying placement.

## 5. Messaging Architecture

**Master message**
> A 16U A player became a Junior A signee — through mindset, mechanics, and consistency. The system that did it is now a platform.

**To parents**
> You're already spending the money. This is the part nobody is coaching: how your kid trains between sessions, and how he thinks when it isn't going well.

**To players**
> You don't need to be the most talented kid on the ice. You need to be the one the coach trusts.

**To coaches/associations**
> Your practices are 60 minutes. This is what your players do with the other 167 hours.

## 6. The Six Pillars (the product's spine — use everywhere)

The framework the whole platform is organized around. Every piece of content maps to exactly one pillar.

| # | Pillar | What it covers | Icon direction |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **MINDSET** | Confidence, bounce-back, adversity, injury recovery, pre-game routine | Brain / spark |
| 2 | **MECHANICS** | Shot technique, the 7-point framework, release, weight transfer | Target |
| 3 | **SKILL** | Hands, edges, puck protection, deception, 1-on-1s | Puck / stick path |

The founder's story maps 1:1 onto these pillars, which is why the framework will never feel invented for marketing.

## 7. Visual Identity

### Colour system

| Token | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|
| `--ink` | `#05070C` | Page background. Near-black, cold. |
| `--navy-900` | `#0A1428` | Section backgrounds, cards |
| `--navy-700` | `#132444` | Elevated surfaces, borders |
| `--electric` | `#0A84FF` | Primary CTA, active states, key accents |
| `--electric-glow` | `#3FA9FF` | Hover, gradients, glow |
| `--silver` | `#C7D0DC` | Body text on dark |
| `--silver-dim` | `#8895A7` | Secondary text, captions |
| `--white` | `#FFFFFF` | Headlines |
| `--ice` | `#E8F1FA` | Light section backgrounds (used sparingly) |

**Ratio discipline: 70% black/navy, 20% silver/white, 10% electric blue.** Electric blue is the most expensive colour on the page — spend it only on the thing you want clicked. A page with blue everywhere reads as cheap; a black page with one blue button reads as expensive.

### Typography

- **Display:** `Anton` or `Archivo Black` — heavy condensed uppercase, tight tracking (`-0.02em`). Headlines only.
- **Body/UI:** `Inter` — 400/500/600/700. Everything else.
- **Numerals:** `Inter` tabular for stats and dashboard figures.
- Google Fonts import: `Anton` + `Inter:wght@400;500;600;700;800`

### Layout language

- Generous vertical rhythm (96–140px section padding on desktop).
- Thin `1px rgba(255,255,255,0.08)` hairlines instead of heavy borders.
- Cards: `--navy-900` fill, hairline border, 16px radius, subtle blue glow on hover.
- Rink-line motif: thin blue horizontal rules as section dividers (the only literal hockey reference permitted).
- Photography: high-contrast, cold-toned, real footage. Grainy rink lighting beats studio polish. **Never stock photography.** A real blurry rink photo outperforms a perfect fake one with this audience.
- Motion: restrained. Fade-and-rise on scroll (0.6s, ease-out), 1px lifts on hover. No bouncing, no confetti, no autoplay sound.

### What "premium" means here
Premium in sports performance is not gold and serif. It is: black, negative space, one accent colour, real footage, and copy that doesn't shout. Think a Junior A team's recruiting page crossed with a high-end training facility — not a supplement ad.

## 8. Proof Assets to Build (priority order)

1. **The 90-second story film.** Founder to camera + game footage + the two injuries. This is the single highest-value asset in the business.
2. **Before/after shot mechanics clip.** Founder's own shot, 16U vs now, side by side, annotated. Undeniable.
3. **The Junior A signing photo/moment.** The proof point in one image.
4. **The alternate captain "A."** Leadership proof, visual and instant.
5. **Annotated pro breakdown sample.** Demonstrates the analysis product for free.
6. **Member before/afters.** The compounding asset — start collecting from member #1.

## 9. Brand Guardrails

- Never promise placement at any level.
- Never use a child's disappointment as an ad hook.
- Never fake scarcity, timers, or testimonials.
- Always disclose that results vary.
- Always make cancellation obvious and one click.
- The kid's wellbeing outranks the conversion rate — permanently, in writing, here.
